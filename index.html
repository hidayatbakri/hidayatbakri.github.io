EfficientNet-B2 for Facial Expression Recognition

This project addresses a key challenge in elementary education: the difficulty for teachers in monitoring students' real-time emotional states, which is crucial for adaptive teaching. I developed a deep learning model to accurately classify children's facial expressions ("happy," "neutral," and "sad"), providing a technical foundation for classroom emotion detection systems.
The core of this research involved implementing the EfficientNet-B2 architecture, leveraging transfer learning from its pre-training on ImageNet. The model was fine-tuned on the AffectNet dataset and rigorously evaluated on the Raf-DB dataset. A significant part of this study was analyzing the impact of different preprocessing techniques, where I compared the standard full-face model against models trained on cropped faces and on separated upper/lower facial regions.
The results were definitive: the standard, full-face model delivered the best performance, achieving 93% accuracy and a 0.95 AUC score. In contrast, the experimental preprocessing techniques significantly degraded performance: the cropped-face model achieved 87% accuracy (0.90 AUC), and the split-face approach dropped to 80% (0.85 AUC). This project concludes that a holistic facial representation is most effective for this task and validates EfficientNet-B2 as a powerful tool for developing real-world adaptive learning technologies.

PREDICTION CUSTOMER CHURN

This end-to-end machine learning project aims to predict customer churn for a telecommunications company. Using the IBM Telco dataset, the primary goal was not only to build an accurate predictive model but also to identify the key factors driving customers to leave.
The workflow included data preprocessing, feature engineering, and a comparative analysis of Logistic Regression and XGBoost models, both achieving a robust ROC-AUC score of ~0.85. The Logistic Regression model was ultimately selected for its high interpretability, balancing predictive power with the ability to provide clear explanations to stakeholders.
Key insights from the model revealed that customers on month-to-month contracts, those with fiber optic internet, and those lacking add-on services like Tech Support were at the highest risk of churning. These findings were translated directly into actionable business strategies, such as offering incentives for annual contracts and creating targeted retention campaigns for high-risk customer segments.

Interactive Sales Dashboard - Superstore Analysis

This project involved creating a dynamic, interactive sales dashboard in Microsoft Excel using the global Superstore dataset. The dashboard analyzes performance from 2014-2017, providing a high-level summary and granular insights through slicers for Date, Category, Region, Segment, and Ship Mode. The end goal was to identify key drivers of profitability and areas for strategic improvement. Before analysis, the data underwent a thorough cleaning process to ensure integrity. This included correcting misspellings (e.g., "Mcallen" to "McAllen"), removing 41 instances of double-spacing, and verifying that the dataset contained no null values or duplicate records. The final dashboard tracks key performance indicators, showing a total Profit Margin of 159% and an Average Order Value (AOV) of $29,878.59 over the four-year period.
The analysis uncovered several key actionable insights. A major finding was the significant unprofitability of the Central region, particularly within the Furniture category. The dashboard clearly visualizes a negative correlation where high average discounts in this specific segment are directly linked to financial losses, with some transactions showing losses as high as -$38.3M. Conversely, the West and East regions were identified as the most profitable, driven primarily by the Consumer and Corporate segments. The Technology category was the single largest contributor to overall profit. A clear seasonal trend emerged, with sales consistently peaking in Q3 and Q4 of each year.
